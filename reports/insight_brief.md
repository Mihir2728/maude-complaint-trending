## Interpretation

Coronary DES (NIQ) report volume fell 61% between 2020 and 2025 (6,362 to 2,499). The decline was shared by every major manufacturer (Boston Scientific −68%, Medtronic −65%, Biotronik −60%, Abbott −44%), and 82% of it came from reports other than those derived from published studies or trials, whose share stayed between 7% and 15% each year; this points to a category-wide change in procedure volumes or reporting practice rather than a single product. Against a stable 2023–2024 baseline, 2025 showed no upward signals but a sustained downward shift (−19% vs baseline; June and November below the LCL). The largest month, March 2020 (766 reports), coincided with a batch of literature and clinical-study reports (25% of that month vs 15% across 2020), mostly coded as injury or death without an identified device problem, which accounts for about half of its excess. Across sampled narratives, the recurring theme is delivery-related malfunctions (failure to cross, stent deformation or dislodgement) in calcified or tortuous lesions, usually without patient harm; nothing here would warrant escalation, but confirming the cause of the decline would need procedure volumes and summary-report counts.

## Limitations

MAUDE is passive surveillance: counts reflect reporting, not incidence, and there is no denominator (units sold or procedures). Reports may be incomplete, duplicated or delayed. Months use the date FDA received the report. A signal is a prompt to investigate, not evidence that a device caused harm.
